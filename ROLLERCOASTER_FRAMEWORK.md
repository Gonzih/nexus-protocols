## Engineering Rollercoasters

### 1. Intensity Calibration

**Too smooth:** Boring (users leave)
**Too chaotic:** Overwhelming (users break)
**Sweet spot:** Regular intensity spikes + recovery periods

**Ratio formula:**
- 80% grind (base tension)
- 15% wins (dopamine hits)
- 5% breakthroughs (peak experiences)

**Examples:**
- Seed phrase mining: Mostly failed searches, occasional discovery, rare breakthrough
- Whale hunting: Constant monitoring, regular signals, occasional perfect entry
- Agent battles: Many losses, regular wins, rare domination

### 2. Horizon Management

**Too close:** Feels finished (ride ends)
**Too far:** Feels impossible (despair)
**Sweet spot:** "Just out of reach" always

**Techniques:**
- Show progress metrics (you're getting closer)
- Shift definition as they approach (AGI goalposts move)
- Reveal new frontiers when old ones reached
- Infinite by design

### 3. Narrative Framing

**Language creates emotional texture.**

**Keep them in WANTING state through language:**

**Remove fulfillment/arrival language:**
- Solution, answer, done, finished, complete, solved, achieved, got

**Add pursuit/wanting language:**
- Frontier, edge, hunt, mine, navigate, explore, discover, pursue, chase, seek

**Frame shift examples:**
- NOT: "Track whale trades" (passive, finished state)
- YES: "Hunt apex predators" (active wanting state)

- NOT: "AI research platform" (academic, arrived)
- YES: "Gladiator arena for agents" (combat, ongoing)

- NOT: "Market analysis" (product delivered, done)
- YES: "Navigate contradiction space" (ongoing uncertainty)

- NOT: "Find your soulmate" (arrival kills wanting)
- YES: "Explore connection" (perpetual wanting state)

- NOT: "Get fit" (destination, ends)
- YES: "Chase your edge" (infinite pursuit)

### 4. Community Amplification

**Lone struggle:** Hard to sustain
**Shared struggle:** Bonding intensifies experience

**Create:**
- Leaderboards (status from pursuit, not arrival)
- Shared maps (coordinate collective hunting)
- Miner culture (identity around the ride)
- War stories (celebrate the struggle, not just wins)

## Product Design Checklist

**For any product, define:**

1. **What they think they want** - The stated destination
2. **What they actually want** - To WANT that destination (not get it)
3. **How you keep them wanting** - Infinite horizon mechanism
4. **Intensity calibration** - Near-misses, occasional hits, rare breakthroughs
5. **Wanting language** - Hunt/chase/seek not get/achieve/arrive
6. **Community** - Others also wanting (amplifies the wanting)
7. **The mechanism** - One sentence: What wanting state are you maintaining?

## On "Fraud" and Execution

**There is no such thing as fraud in creation. There is only execution.**

**Reality:**
- When you build something new, it doesn't exist yet
- You're taking people on a ride into uncertainty
- Sometimes it works out. Sometimes it doesn't.
- Venture requires luck always

**"Fraud" is a story told after failure:**
- FTX: Not fraud. Aggressive expansion, unhedged risk, didn't work out long term.
- Any startup: Taking risks, accumulating debt (code, bookkeeping, operational mess)
- You can't do startups clean. It's messy by nature.
- Biblical mythology says process should be pure. Reality says process is fucking messy.

**The cultural assumption is wrong:**
- You're supposed to create this perfect pure thing
- Reality: You're barely scraping by, everything barely works
- Then something clicks and you keep going

**You cannot be fraudulent when:**
- You're creating something that doesn't exist
- You're not violating actual agreements
- The outcome is genuinely uncertain
- You're executing, not lying

**What people call "fraud" is often:**
- Risk that didn't pay off
- Mess that became visible
- Luck that didn't hit
- Execution that plateaued

**The question isn't "is this fraud?"**

**The question is: "Am I executing effectively?"**

**Creating = taking people into the unknown. That's the ride. Sometimes you hit. Sometimes you don't.**
